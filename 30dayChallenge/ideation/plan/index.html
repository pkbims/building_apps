2How ideation works now: product-first

Adopted in round 13 · how ideation works from Run 4

Your idea from the thread above: start from tools already built for an audience, not from problems. The research has one job: find tools. Then we judge them. A tool that exists already proves who it's for, what problem it solves and (with numbers) whether people pay. We judge 30–40 of them with the same rubric, show you all of them, and bring the top 10 with your own twist.

Today: problem-firstFind complaints → guess a solution → hope people want it. Slow, and every idea is a guess.
Proposed: product-firstFind tools from every source → judge them with the rubric → rebuild the best with 1–2 changes. Faster, and the tool itself shows the demand.

The run briefYou · 2 min

Same brief as now, plus one new question:

  • Who: an audience and its specific groups (e.g. young professionals → new grads, consultants)
  • Build types you can make in a day (web app, extension, skill, automation, browser agent)
  • New: how many tools to analyse (30, 40 or 60), and how many to bring you (10)
  • Confirm and Send

Find the toolsMe · 3 helpers · ~1 hour

This is the one job of the research. A tool that exists already shows who wants it and why, so there's no separate "do people want this?" check. Three helpers each search their group of sources. For every tool they record the same fields: name and link · who it's for · the problem it solves · the core feature · price · proof it's working · launch date · where it was found.

Search words: for each audience group, 5–10 words from their job and daily tasks (e.g. realtor → listing, showing, open house, CRM). Rules: active in the last 12 months, skip giant platforms (ChatGPT, Canva…), only record numbers actually seen. Helpers B and C both use your Chrome, so they run one after the other to avoid the tab clash we had in Run 3.

What each helper is told (shortened):

Audience: {audience + groups}. Find {N/3}+ tools built for them, active in the last 12 months. Sources: {its group of sources}, searching these words: {search words}. For each tool record: name + link, who it's for, the problem in one line, core feature, price, proof it's working (with numbers), launch date, source. Skip giant platforms. Only use numbers you actually saw. Write them to tools-{A|B|C}.md and report the 5 with the strongest proof.

Merge and groupMe · ~10 min

Combine the three lists, merge the same tool found twice, and group tools solving the same problem. Result: 30–40 distinct tools, each with its proof.

Judge every tool with the same rubricMe · ~20 min

  • Gates: can a core version be built in a day? Can a stranger use it on their device in a minute? Does it do more than ChatGPT already does?
  • Scores, 1–3 with a reason: Relatable · Value (now backed by its real price and revenue) · Shareable · Visual
  • Every tool gets a verdict, top 10 or cut, with one line on why

Find your twistMe · ~15 min

For each of the top 10, propose 1–2 changes that make it yours. The usual moves:

  • Free where they charge, or one-time instead of monthly
  • Narrower: one specific group instead of everyone
  • Different form: a Chrome extension or Claude skill instead of a web app
  • Private: runs on their own device, nothing uploaded
  • Combined: two small tools in one

No separate demand check: the tool's own proof (revenue, traffic, attention) already shows it.

You reviewYou · 15 min

The run's page shows:

  • All 30–40 tools analysed in one table: who, problem, price, proof, scores, verdict and why
  • The top 10 as cards: the original tool and its proof, our version (the twist), the one-day build, scores with reasons, Keep/Kill

Kept ideas go to Selected Ideas.

P1: Make product-first the way ideation works?
It's your idea, and I agree: it's faster, it scales, and the demand is already proven. Problem-first stays only as the quick Reddit check in step 5.
P2: How many tools should a run analyse?
More tools means better picks, but a longer table for you to look at.

4The rubric (v3)

TestThe question
Gates: yes or no. One "no" and it's out.
One dayCan the magic moment work in 2–3 hours?
TakeableCan a stranger get it working on their own device in under a minute?
Beyond ChatGPTDoes it do something a chat window can't? It lives where the problem happens, runs by itself, connects to real things and acts, handles video, sites or files, or remembers over time.
Scores: 1–3 each, with a reason. Below 2 on any is out. Top score is 12.
RelatableHow many people in the audience have this problem?
ValueWhat exactly does it give them? It must be medium (2) or high (3).
ShareableWho exactly would they send it to?
VisualIs the result clear on mute in 3 seconds?
