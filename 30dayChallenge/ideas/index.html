3Where ideas come from

Four kinds of signal. A strong idea usually has two of them.

A. Proven demos

AI builds that already went viral. I look for outliers: a small creator whose post got far more likes than usual. That's the idea winning, not the creator's fame.

TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, X, Hacker News

B. Real pain

People asking for a tool: "I wish there was…", "is there an app that…", and complaints in comments. This is where Relatable is proven.

Reddit, YouTube and TikTok comments, Ask HN

C. New powers

Things that just became possible: a new model feature, a trending open-source repo, a paid tool that could be rebuilt free. Being first makes it Surprising.

GitHub Trending, Product Hunt, AI release notes

D. Your life

Your own weekly annoyances. The most relatable source there is, and nobody else has it.

You (section 6)

4The weekly process

The same 7 steps every week, in this order. Steps 1–4 are mine, step 5 is yours.

1. Harvest signals
Who: meTime: ~45 minDone when: 40+ signals saved with links
  • Run a fixed list of searches on every working source, e.g. "built with claude", "AI tool I built", "I automated my…", sorted by top this week.
  • The list is saved in the skill. It grows each week with the searches that found winners.
  • Save every signal with its link, numbers and date. Only the last 30 days count. Old viral posts are stale.
2. Turn signals into ideas
Who: meDone when: ~25 raw ideas
  • Each signal becomes 1–3 ideas in one line: "I built an AI that ___ for ___."
  • Remix rule: change at least one of who it's for, what goes in, or what comes out. Never a 1:1 copy, and credit the original.
  • Merge signals that point at the same idea. Two kinds of signal on one idea is a strong sign.
3. Kill fast
Who: meDone when: only ideas that pass the 3 gates are left
  • Check the rubric's 3 gates first (section 5): one day, takeable, safe.
  • Any "no" kills the idea on the spot. That's cheaper than scoring it.
4. Score strictly, with evidence
Who: meDone when: every survivor has a score and a reason for each test
  • Score each of the 5 appeal tests from 1 to 3 (section 5).
  • Every score needs a link or a reason. "Relatable: 3" means nothing. "Relatable: 3, 4 posts this week asking for exactly this (links)" is a score.
  • Anything below 2 on any test is out.
5. You review
Who: youTime: 15 minDone when: every idea is kept or killed
  • A review page like this one, with one card per idea and Keep or Kill on each.
  • One extra test only you can do: would you be excited to build it tomorrow? If not, kill it.

5The rubric, revisited

What's wrong with the current 8 tests
  • They're all pass/fail. If 20 ideas pass, nothing tells you which one is best.
  • Two kinds of test are mixed. "Can I build it?" (one day, takeable) is a different question from "will people care?" (relatable, surprising).
  • Some overlap. Useful and Relatable both ask about the viewer's problem. Surprising and Shareable both ask about the reaction.
  • Safe is missing. A viral tool that leaks data, fakes a real person, or breaks a platform's rules can get your account banned.
  • No evidence is required, so a test can be passed on a hunch.

7How it becomes a skill

  • Name: idea-bank. You say "fill the idea bank" and it runs steps 1–4, then opens the review page.
  • It keeps 4 files: the search list, the rubric, the bank (every idea card and its status), and a log of what each week found.
  • It runs weekly, on the same day each week, so the bank never runs dry.
  • We build the skill only after running this process by hand once or twice. A skill written before that would just be a guess.
