3The daily loop: an hourly recap

Work for an hour, stop, click through what you did in 1–2 minutes. At the end of the day, the recaps are joined into the long video.

Why this beats talking over clips
  • It's how you already show work: clicking through it live.
  • Nothing to trim. A 1–2 minute take has no dead time.
  • Everything is still fresh in your mind after an hour.
  • The cost: you have to stop every hour, and the video shows results, not the work as it happens.

① Morning (10 min)

  • Write today's task list, however rough.
  • Claude turns it into content/day-03/day-03.md:
    • the tasks, in order
    • what you could show for each one
    • the intro bullets for your first recap

② Every hour (2–5 min)

  1. Stop when your hourly timer goes off.
  2. Say "recap" in any Claude session.
  3. Claude writes hour-01.md:
    • 4–5 bullets: what got done
    • Click-through order: which pages, docs, UI or conversations to open, in what order
    • one lesson or surprise, if there was one
  4. Record in Screen Studio with camera and mic on. Follow the click-through order and talk from the bullets. 1–2 minutes.
  5. Export to content/day-03/hours/hour-01.mp4. No trimming needed.
  6. Back to work.
  • First recap of the day opens with the intro: "Day 3 of 30, app 2 of 10, today I'm…"
  • Last recap of the day ends with the outro: the lesson, and tomorrow.
  • So there are no separate intro and outro recordings.

③ End of day (10 min)

  • Say "edit day-03".
  • Claude joins hour-01…hour-N in order, cuts the ums, adds captions and an "Hour 2: …" title card before each recap.
  • You watch the preview, then say "render".
  • 8 hours of work ≈ 8–16 minutes of video.

④ Short (15 min)

  • Either Claude picks the best 40 seconds from the day's recaps,
  • or you record one 40-sec take from the short bullets.
  • Posted to Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube Shorts and Threads.
How Claude knows what you did across 10 agents
  • Every Claude Code agent keeps a log of its whole conversation in ~/.claude/projects/, with a timestamp on every line. That covers every terminal, herdr pane and worktree.
  • content/hour.py reads the last hour of every log and pulls out, for each agent:
    • what you asked it
    • which files it wrote
    • its own summary of where it got to
    • what it committed to git
  • Claude reads that digest and writes the recap bullets.
  • What it can't see: work done outside Claude Code, like tapping through the app on your phone. Add a line for that when you say "recap".

4The 40-second video: one format, every day

Made after the long video, from the same screen clips: you talk these bullets over 2–3 of them. The long video comes first; the short is its trailer. It never sells the app; the speed of the process does the selling.

SecondsBeatWhat you say
0–3Day count"Day 3 of building 10 apps in 30 days."
3–15What got done2–3 steps from today, said quickly. The speed is the point.

30–40Community"How I did all of it is free in my community, link in bio."
On launch days (roughly every third day), "What got done" becomes a 10-second demo of the finished app. The format stays the same, so there's nothing new to plan.

5The long video, start to finish

  • Hour 1 recap: opens with "Day N of 30, app N of 10, today I'm…", then hour 1.
  • Hours 2…N−1: one recap each, with an "Hour 2: login" title card before it.
  • Last recap: the last hour, then the lesson and tomorrow.
  • Inside every recap: what got done → click through it → the choice you made and what it cost → anything that broke.

7Today's setup

For Screen Studio, an iPhone camera and a separate mic. About 30 minutes, once.

  1. iPhone as the webcam (Continuity Camera): put the iPhone on a stand just above the screen, in landscape, at eye level. The Mac and the iPhone need the same Apple ID, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on.
  2. Light: a window or lamp in front of you, never behind you.
  3. Mic: plug it in and put it about a hand's width from your mouth.
  4. Clean screen: hide desktop icons, turn on Do Not Disturb, and make the editor and terminal font bigger (people watching on a phone need to read it).
  5. Screen Studio: install it and grant Screen Recording, Camera and Microphone permissions. In settings, choose the iPhone as the camera and your external mic as the microphone.
  6. Folders: create content/day-01/hours/.
  7. Test (10 min): say "recap", open hour-01.md, record a 1–2 min click-through in Screen Studio, export to hours/hour-01.mp4. Then say "edit day-01".
  8. Check: can you hear yourself, can you read the screen, did it feel natural?
