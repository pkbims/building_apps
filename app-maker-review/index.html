You want one place that shows where each app is right now, the road it took to get there, and the document produced at each step — so you can orient yourself, and so you can walk someone through it: first I did this, here is the document; then this, here is that document.

This page is not the dashboard. It is where we decide what goes in it, using app_1 (DesignMyRoom, the decor app) as the real example. Nothing gets built and no skill gets touched until you have answered the decisions below.

Job 1 — orientation (you, daily). Where am I on each app, what is in flight, what is the next thing. Wants: current state, all apps, terse, accurate.
Job 2 — the walkthrough (someone else, occasionally). The story of how an app got made, step by step, with the artefact at each step. Wants: one app, chronological, narrated, impressive.

These two pull in different directions — a status board is a snapshot, a walkthrough is a story. D1 is where you choose whether one screen serves both or the dashboard has two views.

The html-worker documents — yes they are saved, but they do not open cleanly

Your second question, answered from the files. Short version: every page is saved forever, and opening one today gives you a broken-looking page unless its server happens to be running.

Why: a review page is a folder — index.html + state.json + server.py — and it is never deleted, so all 14 pages across the four apps still exist. But the page asks its server for /api/state on load, and every render call sits inside that request's success handler: the contents rail, the decision list, the recorded answers, the round badge. There is no fallback. Open spec/index.html by double-clicking it and you get the prose with no navigation, no decisions shown, and dead comment buttons — the 42 answers you recorded are in state.json, unread.

You have already solved this once, by hand. app_1/options_review/reading-copy.html is that page with the fetch replaced by a direct render, the save calls stubbed out, the badge changed to "Read-only copy" and a banner saying taps are not saved. It was made on Sep 11 for reading on your phone. It has never been generalised — it is 30 lines of difference, done once, for one page out of fourteen.
